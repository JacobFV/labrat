Hey, I'm Jacob, and this is LabRat: a desk lab I built today that simulates before it touches, and learns from every run.

Here's my bench: two SO-101 arms, a 3018 CNC carrying a servo pipette I designed this morning, and an iPhone that's the robot's eyes, ears and voice. My laptop sits on the side running a small USB bridge.

The hard part of a physical agent isn't the hardware. It's the harness: memory, safety, and getting better over time. So I architected LabRat around the sponsors' stack.

Everything dials out to one server I deployed on AWS: an EC2 box with HTTPS, so my iPhone, my laptop and the robot all meet in one place with no local networking.

Claude is the brain. When I say "make the most orange well from red and yellow", Claude plans the experiment and calls tools: design, simulate, run, look.

Before anything moves, LabRat simulates sixteen versions of the experiment in a 3D digital twin, picks the best one, and waits for my approval. The model plans; code decides what moves.

MongoDB Atlas is the memory. Every run is stored as one of four memory types: episodic, semantic, procedural and taxonomic. And Voyage AI embeddings let Claude recall what worked last time before it plans the next experiment.

After a real run, the iPhone camera reads the actual colour, LabRat compares it with its prediction and refits its own simulator. In my test, one observation cut prediction error by sixty-nine percent. That's recursive self-improvement, and every step of it is traced in Raindrop, so I can see exactly why the agent did what it did and whether it got better.

ElevenLabs gives LabRat its voice, so I can just talk to it while my hands are busy at the bench.

And Modelence runs my Mission Control dashboard: every session and every event, live.

AWS, Claude, MongoDB, Voyage, Raindrop, ElevenLabs, Modelence: each one doing a real job in the loop.

Think in simulation. Act with permission. Remember everything. This is LabRat.
